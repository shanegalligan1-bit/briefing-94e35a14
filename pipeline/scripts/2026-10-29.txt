Anthropic had a busy few days on two fronts. On the enterprise side, the company rolled out Smart Reports, a new suite that shows businesses what their AI spending is actually producing: team-level breakdowns of what got built, what it cost, and where workflows broke down, plus pooled group budgets so teams can manage token spending more easily. Anthropic says the data won't be used for model training by default. On the science side, Anthropic announced it's acquiring Mithril Medicine, a one-person biotech founded by researcher Cody Tranbarger, who's joining the company to continue muscular dystrophy research using Claude, alongside the half dozen or so drug-discovery partnerships Anthropic already has running. It's an early test of Anthropic's bet that a single researcher paired with AI can run real drug development.

Salesforce is leaning further into its Claude partnership. Since launching Claudeforce back in August, the company has rolled out Salesforce in Claude, a plugin with three dozen or so prebuilt sales skills like meeting prep and pipeline review, with every action still routed through Salesforce so existing business rules and permissions apply. This month Salesforce also started rolling out live-linked dashboards and reports inside Slack that refresh automatically as the underlying data changes, and Agentforce Coworker, the autonomous AI teammate that indexes company data across Salesforce, Slack, and Teams, is now powered by Claude by default.

On the world stage, the big story remains the push toward peace talks over Ukraine. President Zelensky has proposed a three-way summit with the U.S. and Russia for the sixth through tenth of November, and expects Washington to lock in a date just after the U.S. midterm elections. The Kremlin says timing depends on conditions on the battlefield and hasn't confirmed a date yet, with Abu Dhabi the leading candidate for a venue. Meanwhile the Dutch government announced a half a billion euro military aid package for Ukraine, including drones, and called for tougher sanctions on Russia. Back home, Washington avoided a government shutdown this month after a stopgap funding deal, though the next funding cliff lands December eleventh.

In Ireland, the fallout continues from this week's news that thirty-six computers were stolen from the new children's hospital, adding to concerns about overcrowding that's already left hundreds of patients without beds. Separately, the debate over Irish unity is heating up again: Taoiseach Micheál Martin is pushing back against colleagues calling for an early border poll, insisting the Shared Island initiative and cross-border reconciliation need to come first, a stance critics say is leaving him increasingly isolated as pressure for constitutional change builds.

A GoFundMe for her medical costs is up and running, and Kendall Auto Group alone has raised more than one hundred thousand dollars for the Silver Rail community.
